## Distribution is not a single sample

- $p_{data}$: the **distribution** of real data.
- $x \sim p_{data}$: one sampled data point.
- $p_{prior}$: an easy-to-sample prior distribution, typically Gaussian.
- $e \sim p_{prior}$: one noise sample.

“Random” describes the **sampling operation**. It is not a separate distribution.

## Linear probability path

For the MeanFlow / iMF convention:

$$
z_t=(1-t)x+te
$$

Therefore:

$$
t=0 \Rightarrow z_0=x, \qquad t=1 \Rightarrow z_1=e.
$$

<ProbabilityPath lang="en" />

:::note[Training is not rollout]
During training, $x$, $e$, and $t$ are sampled and $z_t$ is **constructed directly** from the equation. The model does not start from Gaussian noise and simulate through all previous times to obtain this training point.
:::

## What if the data distribution changes but the linear path stays the same?

The intermediate distributions do not look the same. Even with the same Gaussian prior and the same formula $z_t=(1-t)x+te$, the geometry of $p_t$ still depends strongly on $p_{data}$.

Use the explorer below to switch among Gaussian, bimodal, ring, checkerboard, two moons, and spiral distributions. Drag $t$ and watch:

- when modes start to blur or connect;
- whether the hole inside a ring is preserved;
- how curved manifolds thicken under noise;
- how disconnected modes mix at intermediate time;
- how one paired sample moves along $x\rightarrow z_t\rightarrow e$.

<DistributionFamilyExplorer lang="en" />

The key intuition is that a probability path is not just one “line.” It is a whole family of time-indexed distributions $p_t$. A single sample interpolation is simple, while the full density geometry can still be complicated.
