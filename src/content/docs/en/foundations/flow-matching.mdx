Keep two points in mind first:

1. training samples $x,e,t$ and constructs $z_t$ directly;
2. for one sampled pair, the supervision target is $e-x$.

## What is marginal velocity actually averaging?

One sampled pair is not enough to explain marginal velocity. The real idea is that **the same or very similar $(z_t,t)$ can be compatible with many sampled pairs under the data-generating process, so the model sees many different conditional targets $e-x$.**

The interactive below uses a small neighborhood rather than forcing several perfectly symmetric arrows onto one exact point. Each white point is a nearby $z_t$ sample, each orange arrow is that sample's own $e-x$, and the purple arrow is their finite-sample average.

<MarginalVelocityIntuition lang="en" />

The key distinction is:

- the training label is still one sample-level $e-x$ at a time;
- marginal velocity is not an extra label supplied by the dataset;
- it is the MSE-optimal single prediction formed from many sample-specific targets under the same conditional state.

The theoretical quantity is

$$
v(z_t,t)=\mathbb E[e-x\mid z_t,t].
$$

For a continuous distribution, exact duplicate $z_t$ values are not something you usually observe repeatedly in a finite table. The conditional expectation is a distribution-level concept. The local neighborhood in the visualization is only an intuitive finite-sample approximation, not the definition itself.

## General formula

From

$$
z_t=(1-t)x+te
$$

we obtain

$$
\frac{dz_t}{dt}=e-x.
$$

For a particular sampled pair $(x,e)$, $e-x$ is the **conditional instantaneous velocity**.

But the marginal model $v_\theta(z_t,t)$ sees only the current state and time, not which sampled pair produced that state. Therefore the unique MSE regression optimum is the conditional mean:

$$
v(z_t,t)=\mathbb E[e-x\mid z_t,t].
$$

## Three “GT” levels that should not be conflated

| Level | Quantity | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Sample supervision | $e-x$ | Conditional velocity for one sampled pair |
| Unique instantaneous field | $v(z,t)$ | Marginal velocity, the MSE regression optimum |
| MeanFlow theoretical field | $u(z,r,t)$ | Average velocity across an interval |

Flow Matching trains

$$
\mathcal L_{FM}=\mathbb E\|v_\theta(z_t,t)-(e-x)\|^2.
$$

At inference time, it numerically solves $dz_t/dt=v_\theta(z_t,t)$ from $t=1$ to $t=0$, typically requiring multiple NFEs.
