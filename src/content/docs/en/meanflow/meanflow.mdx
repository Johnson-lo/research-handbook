## 0. What problem is MeanFlow solving?

Flow Matching models a local instantaneous velocity. As the state moves, the local direction can change, so inference usually queries the network repeatedly. MeanFlow asks whether a finite-time transport quantity can be learned directly so that one network evaluation can move $z_t$ to $z_r$.

## 2. Average velocity

MeanFlow defines

$$
u(z_t,r,t)=\frac{1}{t-r}\int_r^t v(z_\tau,\tau)\,d\tau.
$$

Therefore

$$
z_r=z_t-(t-r)u(z_t,r,t).
$$

For one-step generation, $(r,t)=(0,1)$ gives

$$
z_0=z_1-u_\theta(z_1,0,1).
$$

## 3. Why a special identity is needed

For the linear path $z_t=(1-t)x+te$, the sample-level conditional velocity $e-x$ is cheap to obtain. The interval-average quantity $u(z_t,r,t)$ does not come with an equally convenient per-sample label, which motivates the MeanFlow Identity.

## 5. Deriving the MeanFlow Identity

<DerivationStepper lang="en" />

Differentiating

$$
(t-r)u=\int_r^t v(z_\tau,\tau)d\tau
$$

with respect to $t$ gives

$$
\boxed{v=u+(t-r)\frac{du}{dt}}.
$$

## 6. Why the total derivative is a JVP

Because $u=u(z_t,r,t)$, $r$ is fixed, and $dz_t/dt=v$,

$$
\frac{du}{dt}=\partial_z u\,v+\partial_r u\cdot 0+\partial_tu
=J_{u(z,r,t)}[v,0,1].
$$

MeanFlow abbreviates this as

$$
\operatorname{JVP}(u;v)\equiv J_{u(z,r,t)}[v,0,1].
$$

### Visualize $(z_t,r,t)$ as a 3D input space

<JVP3DGeometry lang="en" />

The crucial distinction is:

- $\partial u/\partial z_t$, $\partial u/\partial r$, and $\partial u/\partial t$ are sensitivities along the three coordinate axes;
- the JVP follows the actual time-evolution direction $q'(t)=[v,0,1]$.

So $\partial u/\partial r$ can absolutely be nonzero. It contributes zero to this particular total derivative only because $r$ is held fixed while differentiating with respect to $t$, hence $dr/dt=0$.

## 7. Original MeanFlow objective

The theory calls for the marginal velocity $v(z_t,t)$, while a training sample gives the conditional velocity $e-x$. Original MF uses $e-x$ as the JVP tangent approximation:

$$
u_{tgt}=(e-x)-(t-r)\operatorname{JVP}(u_\theta;e-x).
$$

and trains

$$
\mathcal L_{MF}=\mathbb E\|u_\theta-\operatorname{sg}(u_{tgt})\|^2.
$$

| Quantity | Meaning | Role |
|---|---|---|
| $e-x$ | sample-specific conditional velocity | supervision + original MF JVP tangent |
| $v(z_t,t)$ | unique marginal instantaneous velocity | field required by the theoretical identity |
| $u(z_t,r,t)$ | interval-average velocity | MeanFlow inference object |

This sample-specific tangent becomes the direct starting point of the [Improved MeanFlow](/research-handbook/en/meanflow/improved-meanflow/) reformulation.
