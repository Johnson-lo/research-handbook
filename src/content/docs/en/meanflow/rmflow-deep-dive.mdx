# 0. Start from the endpoint object RMFlow refines

Let a one-step model produce a deterministic endpoint

$$
\mu_\theta(x_0)=g_\theta(x_0).
$$

RMFlow adds Gaussian refinement

$$
Y=g_\theta(x_0)+\tau\varepsilon,
\qquad
\varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I).
$$

Hence

$$
Y\mid X_0=x_0
\sim
\mathcal N\bigl(g_\theta(x_0),\tau^2I\bigr).
$$

<RMFlowNoiseDemo lang="en" />

The key point is that iMF changes how the deterministic map is trained, whereas RMFlow only needs the final deterministic map itself.

---

# 1. What exactly changes from Original MF to iMF?

For linear interpolation

$$
Z_t=(1-t)X+tE,
$$

let the sample conditional velocity be

$$
C:=E-X.
$$

The state-conditioned marginal velocity is

$$
v^*(z,t)
:=
\mathbb E[C\mid Z_t=z,T=t].
$$

A crucial distinction is:

- the sample label remains $E-X$;
- the population regression target is the conditional mean $v^*(z,t)$;
- iMF changes the **JVP tangent** from the sample-specific $E-X$ to a state-conditioned prediction $v_\theta(z,t)$.

So it is imprecise to say that iMF simply "replaces the target by $v_\theta$."

---

# 2. MeanFlow identity

Let $u^*(z_t,r,t)$ denote the interval-average velocity and define

$$
\Delta=t-r.
$$

The MeanFlow identity can be written as

$$
 u^*(z_t,r,t)
=
v^*(z_t,t)-\Delta\frac{d}{dt}u^*(z_t,r,t).
$$

Therefore

$$
 v^*(z_t,t)
=
 u^*(z_t,r,t)
+
\Delta\operatorname{JVP}(u^*;v^*).
\tag{A}
$$

This exact identity is what makes a state-conditioned JVP tangent theoretically admissible.

---

# 3. Theorem 1 — Original MF $u$-loss equals its $v$-loss reformulation

Original MF uses the apparent target

$$
 u_{\mathrm{tgt}}
=
C-
\Delta\operatorname{JVP}_{\mathrm{sg}}(u_\theta;C).
$$

Its loss is

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{MF}}
=
\mathbb E
\left\|
 u_\theta-\operatorname{sg}(u_{\mathrm{tgt}})
\right\|^2.
$$

Define

$$
V_\theta^{\mathrm{MF}}
:=
 u_\theta
+
\Delta\operatorname{JVP}_{\mathrm{sg}}(u_\theta;C).
$$

Then pointwise

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_\theta^{\mathrm{MF}}-C
&=
 u_\theta
+
\Delta\operatorname{JVP}_{\mathrm{sg}}(u_\theta;C)-C\\
&=
 u_\theta-u_{\mathrm{tgt}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence

$$
\left\|V_\theta^{\mathrm{MF}}-C\right\|^2
=
\left\|u_\theta-u_{\mathrm{tgt}}\right\|^2.
$$

Because the JVP output is stop-gradient in both forms, the gradients with respect to $\theta$ also agree. Thus Original MF is exactly equivalent, under the stated stop-gradient convention, to

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{MF}}
=
\mathbb E
\left\|
V_\theta^{\mathrm{MF}}-(E-X)
\right\|^2.
$$

---

# 4. Theorem 2 — Why sample labels $E-X$ recover marginal velocity

Let

$$
C=E-X,
\qquad
S=(Z_t,T,R),
$$

and assume

$$
\mathbb E[C\mid S]
=
\mathbb E[C\mid Z_t,T]
=
v^*(Z_t,T).
$$

For any measurable predictor $F(S)$ define

$$
\mathcal R(F)=\mathbb E\|F(S)-C\|^2.
$$

Set

$$
m(S):=\mathbb E[C\mid S].
$$

Using the standard orthogonal decomposition,

$$
\boxed{
\mathbb E\|F(S)-C\|^2
=
\mathbb E\|F(S)-m(S)\|^2
+
\mathbb E\|C-m(S)\|^2
}
\tag{B}
$$

because the cross term vanishes by conditional expectation. Therefore the population MSE minimizer is

$$
F^*(S)=m(S)=v^*(Z_t,T)
$$

almost surely.

So although each minibatch label is sample-specific $E-X$, a predictor that only depends on the current state is driven toward the marginal velocity.

---

# 5. Why Original MF is not a standard state-only regression

Original MF uses

$$
V_\theta^{\mathrm{MF}}
=
 u_\theta
+
\Delta\operatorname{JVP}_{\mathrm{sg}}(u_\theta;E-X).
$$

The predictor therefore depends explicitly on the sample-specific conditional velocity. It is effectively

$$
V_\theta^{\mathrm{MF}}(Z_t,E-X,R,T),
$$

not merely a function of state and time. This is the structural issue iMF removes.

---

# 6. Theorem 3 — Why replacing the JVP tangent by $v_\theta$ is population-consistent

iMF defines

$$
V_\theta^{\mathrm{iMF}}(S)
=
 u_\theta(Z_t,R,T)
+
\Delta\operatorname{JVP}_{\mathrm{sg}}
\bigl(u_\theta;v_\theta(Z_t,T)\bigr),
$$

with loss

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{iMF}}
=
\mathbb E
\left\|
V_\theta^{\mathrm{iMF}}(S)-C
\right\|^2.
$$

Now the predictor is state-only with respect to the sample pair. By Theorem 2, its Bayes-optimal population prediction is $v^*(Z_t,T)$.

Assume:

1. $u^*$ and $v^*$ are sufficiently smooth;
2. the exact MeanFlow identity (A) holds;
3. the model class can represent $u^*$ and $v^*$.

Substituting

$$
 u_\theta=u^*,
\qquad
v_\theta=v^*
$$

into the iMF compound predictor gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_\theta^{\mathrm{iMF}}
&=
 u^*
+
\Delta\operatorname{JVP}(u^*;v^*)\\
&=v^*.
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore the exact MeanFlow solution attains the Bayes-optimal population predictor. Equivalently, the tangent replacement does not exclude the correct solution.

This is the rigorous feasibility statement:

> Under the population-limit, representability, smoothness, and exact-identity assumptions, replacing the sample-specific JVP tangent $E-X$ by the marginal tangent $v_\theta$ is consistent with the exact MeanFlow solution.

---

# 7. Boundary construction

From the interval-average definition,

$$
 u^*(z_t,r,t)
=
\frac{1}{t-r}
\int_r^t v^*(z_\tau,\tau)d\tau.
$$

If $v^*$ is continuous, then as $r\to t$,

$$
\boxed{
 u^*(z_t,t,t)=v^*(z_t,t)
}
\tag{C}
$$

so the boundary parameterization

$$
v_\theta(z_t,t):=u_\theta(z_t,t,t)
$$

is consistent with the exact solution.

---

# 8. Auxiliary $v$-head

If iMF uses a separate auxiliary head, the standard FM auxiliary objective is

$$
\mathcal L_v
=
\mathbb E
\left\|
v_\theta(Z_t,T)-(E-X)
\right\|^2.
$$

By Theorem 2 its population minimizer is

$$
v_\theta^*(Z_t,T)
=
\mathbb E[E-X\mid Z_t,T]
=
v^*(Z_t,T).
$$

Thus the auxiliary head has an explicit Bayes regression target.

---

# 9. RMFlow endpoint likelihood still holds after the iMF change

Use a generic deterministic one-step endpoint map

$$
G_\theta(S_0).
$$

Depending on sign/time convention, this may be written as either

$$
G_\theta(z_1)=z_1-u_\theta(z_1,0,1)
$$

or

$$
G_\theta(x_0)=x_0+\hat u_{0,1}(x_0;\theta).
$$

RMFlow defines

$$
Y=G_\theta(S_0)+\tau\varepsilon,
\qquad
\varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I).
$$

Hence

$$
Y\mid S_0=s
\sim
\mathcal N\bigl(G_\theta(s),\tau^2I\bigr),
$$

and

$$
-\log p_\theta(y\mid s)
=
\frac{\|y-G_\theta(s)\|^2}{2\tau^2}
+
\frac d2\log(2\pi\tau^2).
$$

For fixed $\tau$, define

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{endpoint}}
:=
\mathbb E
\|Y-G_\theta(S_0)\|^2.
$$

Jensen then yields

$$
-\frac{1}{2\tau^2}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{endpoint}}+C
\le
\mathbb E_Y[\log p_\theta(Y)],
$$

and

$$
\mathbb E_{p_{\mathrm{tgt}}}[\log p_\theta(Y)]
=
-H(p_{\mathrm{tgt}})
-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\mathrm{tgt}}\|p_\theta).
$$

This proof depends only on the final deterministic map and Gaussian refinement. It never uses the internal JVP tangent. Therefore the iMF replacement does not invalidate the RMFlow endpoint-likelihood theorem.

---

# 10. Endpoint-gradient theorem

If $G_\theta$ is differentiable and gradient/expectation interchange is valid,

$$
\nabla_\theta\mathcal L_{\mathrm{endpoint}}
=
2\mathbb E
\left[
J_{G_\theta}(S_0)^T
\bigl(G_\theta(S_0)-Y\bigr)
\right].
$$

Therefore the endpoint NLL directly updates the actual inference map.

For the boundary construction, the same backbone parameters define $u_\theta$ and $v_\theta$. For a separate auxiliary head, the endpoint loss need not directly update head-specific layers; those layers are primarily trained by $\mathcal L_v$.

---

# 11. A mathematically valid hybrid objective

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{hybrid}}
=
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{iMF}}
+
\beta\mathcal L_v
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{NLL}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{endpoint}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{guide}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{guide}}.
$$

The terms control different objects:

- $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{iMF}}$: probability-path regression toward the marginal instantaneous velocity;
- $\mathcal L_v$: direct supervision of an auxiliary marginal-velocity head;
- $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{endpoint}}$: likelihood control on the one-step endpoint;
- $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{guide}}$: optional prior/guidance regularization.

---

# 12. What is proved, and what is not

The page now proves:

1. Original MF $u$-loss and its $v$-loss reformulation are equivalent under the stated stop-gradient convention.
2. State-only MSE regression on sample labels $E-X$ has population optimum $v^*(z_t,t)$.
3. Under exact representability and the MeanFlow identity, $(u^*,v^*)$ is a population-optimal iMF solution after replacing the JVP tangent by $v_\theta$.
4. RMFlow Gaussian NLL / Jensen / forward-KL reasoning remains valid for the iMF endpoint map.

It does **not** yet prove an iMF analogue of the original CMFM Wasserstein bound. For such a result one still needs a stability inequality connecting iMF regression error to endpoint-map error.

A useful conditional route is: if one can establish

$$
\mathbb E
\|G_\theta(S_0)-G_*(S_0)\|^2
\le
C\mathcal L_{\mathrm{iMF}},
$$

then the same-source coupling gives

$$
W_2^2
\bigl((G_\theta)_\#q,(G_*)_\#q\bigr)
\le
\mathbb E
\|G_\theta(S_0)-G_*(S_0)\|^2.
$$

Adding the same Gaussian noise to both sides does not enlarge this shared-noise coupling bound.

---

# 13. Final logic chain

Original MF:

$$
C=E-X
\longrightarrow
u_{\mathrm{tgt}}
=
C-\Delta\operatorname{JVP}(u_\theta;C).
$$

Equivalent $v$-loss view:

$$
V_\theta^{\mathrm{MF}}(Z_t,C)
=
 u_\theta
+
\Delta\operatorname{JVP}(u_\theta;C)
\rightarrow C.
$$

iMF:

$$
V_\theta^{\mathrm{iMF}}(Z_t)
=
 u_\theta
+
\Delta\operatorname{JVP}(u_\theta;v_\theta(Z_t)),
$$

still supervised by sample labels

$$
C=E-X,
$$

whose state-conditioned population target is

$$
v^*(Z_t)=\mathbb E[E-X\mid Z_t].
$$

RMFlow then acts on the inference endpoint

$$
S_0
\xrightarrow{\;G_\theta\;}
\text{one-step mean}
\xrightarrow{+\tau\varepsilon}
Y.
$$

So the three levels are distinct:

> sample supervision is $E-X$; the iMF JVP tangent is $v_\theta$; RMFlow NLL acts on the final endpoint map $G_\theta$.
