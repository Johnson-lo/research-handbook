# Part I — Original RMFlow method

# 1. What problem does RMFlow address?

MeanFlow aims to perform transport with a single neural-function evaluation. The difficulty is that one deterministic step must place a sample near a valid endpoint of a multimodal target distribution immediately.

RMFlow keeps the one-step MeanFlow transport and appends a cheap Gaussian refinement:

$$
\text{prior}
\xrightarrow{\text{1-NFE MeanFlow}}
\text{coarse intermediate endpoint}
\xrightarrow{\text{Gaussian refinement}}
\text{final sample}.
$$

The objective is to retain transport-side control while adding endpoint density / likelihood control.

---

# 2. Equation (5): transport-side control

RMFlow cites an existing MeanFlow / flow-map result of the form

$$
M\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CMFM}}(\theta)
\ge
W_2^2(p_{\mathrm{tgt}},p_\theta),
\qquad M>0.
\tag{5}
$$

where

$$
W_2^2(p_{\mathrm{tgt}},p_\theta)
=
\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(p_{\mathrm{tgt}},p_\theta)}
\mathbb E_{(x,y)\sim\gamma}\|x-y\|^2.
$$

This controls transport geometry.

:::caution[Equation (5) is not newly proved by RMFlow]
The new RMFlow theory concerns the additional likelihood / forward-KL direction developed below.
:::

---

# 3. Equations (6)–(8): why the two noise stages produce $\sigma_{\min}$

The first-stage target is

$$
x_1
=
x_{\mathrm{data}}+\sigma\epsilon_1,
\qquad
\epsilon_1\sim\mathcal N(0,I),
\qquad
\sigma<\sigma_{\min}.
\tag{6}
$$

The second stage adds

$$
x_{\mathrm{tgt}}
=
x_1+
\sqrt{\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2}\,\epsilon_2,
\qquad
\epsilon_2\sim\mathcal N(0,I).
\tag{7}
$$

If $\epsilon_1\perp\epsilon_2$, then

$$
x_{\mathrm{tgt}}
=
x_{\mathrm{data}}
+\sigma\epsilon_1
+\sqrt{\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2}\,\epsilon_2.
$$

Independent Gaussian covariances add:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}
\left[
\sigma\epsilon_1+
\sqrt{\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2}\,\epsilon_2
\right]
&=
\sigma^2I+(\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2)I\\
&=
\sigma_{\min}^2I.
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence there exists a standard Gaussian $\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$ such that

$$
x_{\mathrm{tgt}}
\overset d=
x_{\mathrm{data}}+\sigma_{\min}\epsilon.
$$

The quantity that adds is variance:

$$
\sigma^2+(\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2)
=
\sigma_{\min}^2.
$$

## 3.1 Generation equation: Equation (8)

The learned first-stage endpoint is

$$
x_1=x_0+\hat u_{0,1}(x_0;\theta).
$$

After the Gaussian refinement,

$$
\hat x_{\mathrm{tgt}}
=
x_0+\hat u_{0,1}(x_0;\theta)
+
\sqrt{\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2}\,\epsilon_2.
\tag{8}
$$

The refinement requires no second neural evaluation, so generation remains 1-NFE.

---

# 4. Equation (9): the conditional Gaussian and log-likelihood

Define

$$
\mu_\theta(x_0)
:=
x_0+\hat u_{0,1}(x_0;\theta),
$$

and

$$
\tau^2
:=
\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2>0.
$$

Then

$$
\hat x_{\mathrm{tgt}}
=
\mu_\theta(x_0)+\tau\epsilon_2,
\qquad
\epsilon_2\sim\mathcal N(0,I).
$$

Conditioning on $x_0$ gives

$$
\hat x_{\mathrm{tgt}}\mid x_0
\sim
\mathcal N(\mu_\theta(x_0),\tau^2I).
$$

Therefore

$$
p_\theta(y\mid x_0)
=
\frac{1}{(2\pi\tau^2)^{d/2}}
\exp\left[
-\frac{\|y-\mu_\theta(x_0)\|^2}{2\tau^2}
\right].
$$

Taking logs,

$$
\log p_\theta(y\mid x_0)
=
-\frac{1}{2\tau^2}
\|y-\mu_\theta(x_0)\|^2
-
\frac d2\log(2\pi\tau^2).
\tag{9}
$$

For later convenience define

$$
A:=\frac{1}{2\tau^2}>0,
\qquad
B:=\frac d2\log(2\pi\tau^2).
$$

Then

$$
-\log p_\theta(y\mid x_0)
=
A\|y-\mu_\theta(x_0)\|^2+B.
$$

---

# 5. Equation (10): is the paper's $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}$ literally the NLL?

Using

$$
y=x_{\mathrm{tgt}}
\overset d=
x_{\mathrm{data}}+\sigma_{\min}\epsilon,
$$

RMFlow defines

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}
:=
\mathbb E_{x_0,x_{\mathrm{data}},\epsilon}
\left[
\left\|
(x_{\mathrm{data}}+\sigma_{\min}\epsilon)
-
(x_0+\hat u_{0,1}(x_0;\theta))
\right\|^2
\right].
\tag{10}
$$

By Equation (9), the true **expected conditional Gaussian NLL** is

$$
\mathbb E[-\log p_\theta(Y\mid X_0)]
=
A\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}+B.
\tag{10a}
$$

Thus Equation (10) is the MSE surrogate obtained after dropping the fixed positive scale $A$ and additive constant $B$.

When $\tau$ is fixed,

$$
\arg\min_\theta
\mathbb E[-\log p_\theta(Y\mid X_0)]
=
\arg\min_\theta
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}.
$$

If $\tau$ is learnable, this equivalence no longer holds because both $A$ and $B$ depend on model parameters.

# Part II — NLL, Cross-Entropy, and KL: the missing probability-theory bridge

# 6. Cross-entropy definition: NLL and cross-entropy live at different levels

Let the target density be

$$
p(y)=p_{\mathrm{tgt}}(y),
$$

and the model marginal density be

$$
q_\theta(y)=p_\theta(y).
$$

Assume:

1. $p$ and $q_\theta$ admit densities with respect to the same base measure;
2. $q_\theta(y)>0$ for $p$-almost every $y$;
3. $\mathbb E_p[|\log q_\theta(Y)|]<\infty$.

The **cross-entropy** is

$$
H_\times(p,q_\theta)
:=
-\int p(y)\log q_\theta(y)\,dy
=
\mathbb E_{Y\sim p}
[-\log q_\theta(Y)].
\tag{CE-1}
$$

For a single sample, the negative log-likelihood is

$$
\ell_{\mathrm{NLL}}(y;\theta)
:=
-\log q_\theta(y).
$$

Therefore the precise relation is

$$
H_\times(p,q_\theta)
=
\mathbb E_{Y\sim p}
[\ell_{\mathrm{NLL}}(Y;\theta)].
\tag{CE-2}
$$

So:

> **NLL is a per-sample loss; cross-entropy is the population expectation of that NLL under the target distribution.**

---

# 7. Why empirical average NLL is a Monte Carlo estimator of cross-entropy

Let

$$
Y_1,\ldots,Y_n\overset{iid}{\sim}p.
$$

The empirical marginal NLL is

$$
\widehat{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{NLL}}^{\mathrm{marg}}
:=
-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\log q_\theta(Y_i).
\tag{CE-3}
$$

Taking expectation,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E
\left[
\widehat{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{NLL}}^{\mathrm{marg}}
\right]
&=
-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\mathbb E_{Y_i\sim p}[\log q_\theta(Y_i)]\\
&=
-\mathbb E_{Y\sim p}[\log q_\theta(Y)]\\
&=
H_\times(p,q_\theta).
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus empirical average NLL is an unbiased Monte Carlo estimator of marginal cross-entropy.

Under the usual integrability conditions, the strong law of large numbers also gives

$$
\widehat{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{NLL}}^{\mathrm{marg}}
\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{a.s.}
H_\times(p,q_\theta).
$$

---

# 8. Why cross-entropy equals entropy plus forward KL

Target entropy is

$$
H(p)
:=
-\int p(y)\log p(y)dy.
$$

Forward KL is

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q_\theta)
:=
\int p(y)
\log\frac{p(y)}{q_\theta(y)}dy.
$$

Expanding,

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q_\theta)
&=
\int p(y)\log p(y)dy
-
\int p(y)\log q_\theta(y)dy\\
&=
-H(p)+H_\times(p,q_\theta).
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence

$$
H_\times(p,q_\theta)
=
H(p)
+
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q_\theta).
\tag{CE-4}
$$

Because $p=p_{\mathrm{tgt}}$ is fixed, $H(p)$ is constant with respect to $\theta$. Therefore

$$
\arg\min_\theta H_\times(p,q_\theta)
=
\arg\min_\theta D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q_\theta).
$$

This is the exact maximum-likelihood / cross-entropy / forward-KL equivalence at the population level.

---

# 9. Crucial distinction: RMFlow Equation (10) is not the marginal cross-entropy

RMFlow has a latent prior variable $X_0\sim q_0$ and a conditional model

$$
p_\theta(y\mid x_0).
$$

The marginal model is

$$
p_\theta(y)
=
\int q_0(x_0)p_\theta(y\mid x_0)dx_0.
\tag{CE-5}
$$

In the unguided product-measure setting define the averaged conditional NLL

$$
\mathcal C_{\mathrm{cond}}(\theta)
:=
\mathbb E_{Y\sim p_{\mathrm{tgt}},\,X_0\sim q_0}
[-\log p_\theta(Y\mid X_0)].
\tag{CE-6}
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\mathcal C_{\mathrm{cond}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb E_{X_0\sim q_0}
\left[
H_\times
\bigl(
 p_{\mathrm{tgt}},
 p_\theta(\cdot\mid X_0)
\bigr)
\right].
$$

By the Gaussian Equation (9),

$$
\mathcal C_{\mathrm{cond}}(\theta)
=
A\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}+B.
\tag{CE-7}
$$

So the precise interpretation of RMFlow Equation (10) is:

> **At fixed variance, it is equivalent to minimizing averaged conditional cross-entropy, not directly the marginal cross-entropy $H_\times(p_{\mathrm{tgt}},p_\theta)$.**

---

# 10. Jensen: why conditional NLL upper-bounds marginal cross-entropy

From

$$
p_\theta(y)
=
\mathbb E_{X_0\sim q_0}
[p_\theta(y\mid X_0)],
$$

concavity of $\log$ gives

$$
\log p_\theta(y)
\ge
\mathbb E_{X_0}
[\log p_\theta(y\mid X_0)].
$$

Multiplying by $-1$,

$$
-\log p_\theta(y)
\le
\mathbb E_{X_0}
[-\log p_\theta(y\mid X_0)].
$$

Averaging over $Y\sim p_{\mathrm{tgt}}$ yields

$$
H_\times(p_{\mathrm{tgt}},p_\theta)
\le
\mathcal C_{\mathrm{cond}}(\theta)
=
A\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}+B.
\tag{CE-8}
$$

Thus, in negative-log / cross-entropy language:

> **RMFlow's conditional NLL surrogate is an upper bound on marginal cross-entropy.**

In log-likelihood language, the same fact appears as the lower bound in Equation (11).

---

# 11. Stronger result: the Jensen gap is exactly a posterior KL

Define the model joint density

$$
p_\theta(x_0,y)
:=
q_0(x_0)p_\theta(y\mid x_0).
$$

Bayes' rule gives

$$
p_\theta(x_0\mid y)
=
\frac{q_0(x_0)p_\theta(y\mid x_0)}{p_\theta(y)}.
\tag{CE-9}
$$

Now consider

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\bigl(
q_0(x_0)
\|p_\theta(x_0\mid y)
\bigr).
$$

Substitute the posterior explicitly:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\bigl(q_0\|p_\theta(\cdot\mid y)\bigr)\\
&=
\int q_0(x_0)
\log
\frac{q_0(x_0)}
{p_\theta(x_0\mid y)}dx_0\\
&=
\int q_0(x_0)
\log
\frac{q_0(x_0)}
{q_0(x_0)p_\theta(y\mid x_0)/p_\theta(y)}dx_0\\
&=
\int q_0(x_0)
\left[
\log p_\theta(y)
-
\log p_\theta(y\mid x_0)
\right]dx_0\\
&=
\log p_\theta(y)
-
\mathbb E_{X_0\sim q_0}
[\log p_\theta(y\mid X_0)].
\end{aligned}
\tag{CE-10}
$$

Therefore, pointwise in $y$,

$$
\mathbb E_{X_0}
[-\log p_\theta(y\mid X_0)]
=
-\log p_\theta(y)
+
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\bigl(q_0\|p_\theta(\cdot\mid y)\bigr).
\tag{CE-11}
$$

Averaging over $Y\sim p_{\mathrm{tgt}}$ gives

$$
\mathcal C_{\mathrm{cond}}(\theta)
=
H_\times(p_{\mathrm{tgt}},p_\theta)
+
\mathbb E_{Y\sim p_{\mathrm{tgt}}}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\bigl(q_0\|p_\theta(X_0\mid Y)\bigr).
\tag{CE-12}
$$

Since KL is nonnegative, Equation (CE-8) follows immediately.

This identity also shows exactly why the Jensen bound can be loose: the gap is

$$
\mathbb E_Y
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\bigl(q_0\|p_\theta(X_0\mid Y)\bigr).
$$

---

# 12. The full population decomposition of RMFlow Equation (10)

Combining Equations (CE-7), (CE-12), and (CE-4),

$$
\begin{aligned}
A\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}+B
&=
\mathcal C_{\mathrm{cond}}(\theta)\\
&=
H_\times(p_{\mathrm{tgt}},p_\theta)
+
\mathbb E_Y
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\bigl(q_0\|p_\theta(X_0\mid Y)\bigr)\\
&=
H(p_{\mathrm{tgt}})
+
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\mathrm{tgt}}\|p_\theta)\\
&\quad+
\mathbb E_Y
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\bigl(q_0\|p_\theta(X_0\mid Y)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{CE-13}
$$

This is the key identity of the page.

It says that the fixed-variance RMFlow Equation (10) contains:

1. target entropy $H(p_{\mathrm{tgt}})$, constant in $\theta$;
2. the forward KL we would like to reduce,
   $$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\mathrm{tgt}}\|p_\theta);$$
3. the Jensen / latent-posterior gap,
   $$\mathbb E_Y D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_0\|p_\theta(X_0\mid Y)).$$

Therefore minimizing Equation (10) is not literally the same as minimizing marginal forward KL; it minimizes

$$
\text{forward KL}
+
\text{posterior Jensen gap}
$$

up to the fixed entropy constant and fixed Gaussian scale.

---

# 13. Reinterpreting Equations (11), (13)–(17)

From Equation (CE-13),

$$
A\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}+B
\ge
H(p_{\mathrm{tgt}})
+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\mathrm{tgt}}\|p_\theta).
$$

Multiplying by $-1$,

$$
-A\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}-B
\le
-H(p_{\mathrm{tgt}})
-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\mathrm{tgt}}\|p_\theta).
$$

Because

$$
-B
=-\frac d2\log(2\pi\tau^2),
$$

which is the additive log-likelihood constant $C$, we recover

$$
-A\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}+C
\le
\mathbb E_{p_{\mathrm{tgt}}}
[\log p_\theta(Y)]
=
-H(p_{\mathrm{tgt}})
-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\mathrm{tgt}}\|p_\theta).
\tag{11}
$$

So Equation (11) can be read as Equation (CE-13) after dropping the nonnegative posterior-KL gap.

Equations (13)–(16) establish the same inequality via Jensen; Equation (17) is the log-likelihood version of the cross-entropy / KL decomposition.

---

# 14. Important correction: lowering RMFlow conditional NLL does not imply marginal cross-entropy decreases at every optimizer step

Because

$$
\mathcal C_{\mathrm{cond}}
=
H_\times(p_{\mathrm{tgt}},p_\theta)
+
\text{JensenGap}_\theta,
$$

and the Jensen gap itself depends on $\theta$, a decrease in

$$
\mathcal C_{\mathrm{cond}}
$$

does not by itself imply that every optimizer step satisfies

$$
H_\times(p_{\mathrm{tgt}},p_\theta)\downarrow.
$$

The rigorous statement is that RMFlow minimizes an **upper bound on marginal cross-entropy**, equivalently maximizing a **lower bound on marginal expected log-likelihood**.

# 15. Conditional / guided setting

With condition $c$, the rigorous formulation is

$$
q_\omega(x_0\mid c),
\qquad
p_{\mathrm{tgt}}(y\mid c),
$$

and

$$
p_\theta(y\mid c)
=
\int q_\omega(x_0\mid c)
p_\theta(y\mid x_0,c)dx_0.
$$

For each fixed $c$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E_{Y\sim p_{\mathrm{tgt}}(\cdot\mid c),
X_0\sim q_\omega(\cdot\mid c)}
[-\log p_\theta(Y\mid X_0,c)]\\
&=
H_\times
\bigl(
p_{\mathrm{tgt}}(\cdot\mid c),
p_\theta(\cdot\mid c)
\bigr)\\
&\quad+
\mathbb E_Y
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\left(
q_\omega(X_0\mid c)
\|p_\theta(X_0\mid Y,c)
\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

One may then average over $c$.

This is stricter than assuming all variables are unconditionally independent.

# 16. Equation (12): the original RMFlow joint objective

RMFlow optimizes

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{RMFlow}}(\theta,\omega)
=
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CMFM}}
+
\lambda_1\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}
+
\lambda_2
\mathbb E_{(x_{\mathrm{data}},c)}
\|\phi_\omega(c)\|^2.
\tag{12}
$$

| Term | Role in the paper |
|---|---|
| $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CMFM}}$ | MeanFlow path / transport control |
| $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}$ | averaged conditional cross-entropy surrogate; equivalently an upper bound on marginal CE |
| $\|\phi_\omega(c)\|^2$ | guided-prior regularization |

The original first term is CMFM, not iMF.

# Part III — iMF + RMFlow research extension

# 17. What does it really mean to say iMF "changes the target to $v$"?

iMF uses

$$
z_t=(1-t)x+te.
$$

The sample conditional velocity is

$$
v_c=e-x.
$$

The marginal velocity is

$$
v(z_t,t)
=
\mathbb E[e-x\mid z_t,t].
$$

Therefore:

- sample supervision remains $e-x$;
- the population MSE target is marginal $v(z_t,t)$;
- iMF actually replaces the JVP tangent $e-x$ by $v_\theta(z_t,t)$.

---

# 18. Exact reformulation from the Original MF $u$-loss to a $v$-loss

The MeanFlow identity is

$$
 u(z_t,r,t)
=
v(z_t,t)
-(t-r)\frac{d}{dt}u(z_t,r,t).
$$

Hence

$$
v(z_t,t)
=
 u(z_t,r,t)
+(t-r)\operatorname{JVP}(u;v).
$$

Original MF uses the sample tangent $e-x$ and defines

$$
 u_{\mathrm{tgt}}
=
(e-x)
-(t-r)
\operatorname{JVP}_{\mathrm{sg}}(u_\theta;e-x).
$$

If

$$
V_\theta^{\mathrm{MF}}
=
 u_\theta
+(t-r)
\operatorname{JVP}_{\mathrm{sg}}(u_\theta;e-x),
$$

then

$$
V_\theta^{\mathrm{MF}}-(e-x)
=
 u_\theta-u_{\mathrm{tgt}}.
$$

The squared residuals are therefore identical, and under the same stop-gradient convention their gradients are identical as well.

---

# 19. Why the state-only MSE optimum of sample label $e-x$ is marginal $v$

Let

$$
C=e-x,
\qquad
m(z_t,t)=\mathbb E[C\mid z_t,t].
$$

For any state-only predictor $F(z_t,t)$,

$$
\mathbb E\|F-C\|^2
=
\mathbb E\|F-m\|^2
+
\mathbb E\|C-m\|^2.
$$

Indeed, writing $C=m+\xi$ with $\mathbb E[\xi\mid z_t,t]=0$ makes the cross term vanish in expectation.

Therefore the population MSE optimum is

$$
F^*(z_t,t)
=m(z_t,t)
=
v(z_t,t).
$$

---

# 20. How far can the iMF tangent replacement be proved?

Assume exact fields $(u^*,v^*)$:

1. exist and are sufficiently smooth;
2. satisfy the MeanFlow identity;
3. are representable by the model class.

If

$$
 u_\theta=u^*,
\qquad
v_\theta=v^*,
$$

then

$$
V_\theta^{\mathrm{iMF}}
=
 u^*
+(t-r)\operatorname{JVP}(u^*;v^*)
=
v^*.
$$

Since $v^*$ is the Bayes-optimal state-only regression target, the rigorous conclusion is:

> **Under population / exact-representability assumptions, the iMF parameterization does not exclude the exact MeanFlow solution.**

This is a realizability / consistency statement, not an SGD convergence theorem.

---

# 21. If iMF replaces RMFlow's first stage, the target must become the intermediate $p_\sigma$

RMFlow's first-stage target is

$$
p_\sigma
=
p_{\mathrm{data}}*\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I),
$$

not the final

$$
p_{\mathrm{tgt}}
=
p_{\mathrm{data}}*\mathcal N(0,\sigma_{\min}^2I).
$$

Therefore the iMF data-side endpoint should be

$$
X_\sigma
=
X_{\mathrm{data}}+\sigma\epsilon_1.
$$

Under the iMF convention,

$$
Z_t=(1-t)X_\sigma+tE,
$$

so the sample conditional velocity becomes

$$
C_\sigma=E-X_\sigma,
$$

and the marginal velocity is

$$
v_\sigma(z_t,t)
=
\mathbb E[E-X_\sigma\mid Z_t=z_t,t].
$$

A properly matched first-stage loss is therefore

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{iMF}}^{(\sigma)}
=
\mathbb E
\left\|
V_\theta^{\mathrm{iMF}}(Z_t)
-
(E-X_\sigma)
\right\|^2.
$$

# 22. Exact distribution case: first stage to $p_\sigma$, second stage exactly to $p_{\mathrm{tgt}}$

If an exact map $G^*$ satisfies

$$
G^*_{\#}q=p_\sigma,
$$

and the second stage adds

$$
\tau\epsilon_2,
\qquad
\tau^2=\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2,
$$

then

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{final}}
&=
p_\sigma*\mathcal N(0,\tau^2I)\\
&=
p_{\mathrm{data}}
*\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I)
*\mathcal N(0,\tau^2I)\\
&=
p_{\mathrm{data}}
*\mathcal N(0,\sigma_{\min}^2I)\\
&=
p_{\mathrm{tgt}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This is exact distribution-level compatibility.

---

# 23. Approximate endpoint case: does the RMFlow cross-entropy / likelihood proof survive?

Write the one-step endpoint generically as

$$
G_\theta(S_0).
$$

RMFlow refinement is

$$
Y=G_\theta(S_0)+\tau\epsilon_2.
$$

Thus

$$
Y\mid S_0=s
\sim
\mathcal N(G_\theta(s),\tau^2I),
$$

and

$$
-\log p_\theta(y\mid s)
=
\frac{\|y-G_\theta(s)\|^2}{2\tau^2}+B.
$$

This does not depend on whether $G_\theta$ was trained by CMFM or iMF.

Therefore the full Part II decomposition remains valid:

$$
A\mathcal L_{\mathrm{endpoint}}+B
=
H(p_{\mathrm{tgt}})
+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\mathrm{tgt}}\|p_\theta)
+
\mathbb E_Y
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\bigl(q_0\|p_\theta(S_0\mid Y)\bigr),
$$

provided the same prior / marginalization setup and integrability assumptions are used.

So **the iMF replacement does not break the Gaussian conditional NLL → cross-entropy → KL probability chain.**

# 25. Strict separation: proved vs. assumption-dependent vs. open

## Established by RMFlow / standard probability identities

- Gaussian construction in RMFlow Eqs. (6)–(10);
- population expectation of NLL = cross-entropy;
- cross-entropy = entropy + forward KL;
- RMFlow averaged conditional NLL = marginal cross-entropy + expected posterior-KL Jensen gap;
- therefore Eq. (10) is an upper bound on marginal CE, while Eq. (11) is a lower bound on marginal expected log-likelihood;
- an exact first-stage $p_\sigma$ followed by the Gaussian refinement yields $p_{\mathrm{tgt}}$.

## Derivable under explicit assumptions

- Original MF $u$-loss and $v$-loss reformulation are equivalent;
- state-only regression on $e-x$ has marginal $v$ as the population target;
- exact representable $(u^*,v^*)$ can realize the Bayes-optimal iMF solution;
- attaching RMFlow to an iMF endpoint preserves the conditional Gaussian / CE / KL decomposition.

## Still not proved

1. finite-data / finite-network / SGD iMF necessarily reaches exact $p_\sigma$;
2. the original RMFlow bound
   $$M\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CMFM}}\ge W_2^2$$
   remains true after simply replacing CMFM by iMF;
3. every optimizer step that lowers RMFlow conditional NLL also lowers marginal cross-entropy;
4. the hybrid necessarily improves FID or perceptual quality.

# 26. Complete mathematical chain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}^{\mathrm{paper}}
&\xrightarrow{\times A\,+B}
\mathbb E[-\log p_\theta(Y\mid X_0)]\\
&=
H_\times(p_{\mathrm{tgt}},p_\theta)
+
\mathbb E_Y
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_0\|p_\theta(X_0\mid Y))\\
&=
H(p_{\mathrm{tgt}})
+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\mathrm{tgt}}\|p_\theta)
+
\text{Jensen gap}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The most precise summary is:

> **RMFlow Equation (10) is not equal to marginal cross-entropy. At fixed Gaussian variance, it is equivalent to averaged conditional cross-entropy, and that quantity equals marginal cross-entropy plus a nonnegative posterior-KL Jensen gap.**
