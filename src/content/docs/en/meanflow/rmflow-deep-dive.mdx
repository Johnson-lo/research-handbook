# Part I — Original RMFlow method and equations

# 1. What problem does RMFlow address?

MeanFlow aims to generate with a single neural-function evaluation by learning an interval-averaged velocity. The difficulty is that a one-step deterministic map must place the sample close to the correct multimodal endpoint immediately.

RMFlow keeps the one-step MeanFlow transport, but appends a cheap Gaussian refinement:

$$
\text{prior}
\xrightarrow{\text{1-NFE MeanFlow}}
\text{coarse endpoint}
\xrightarrow{\text{Gaussian noise}}
\text{final sample}.
$$

The goal is to retain transport-style control from MeanFlow while adding an endpoint likelihood / KL criterion.

---

# 2. Equation (5): the original transport-side control

RMFlow cites an existing MeanFlow / flow-map result of the form

$$
M\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CMFM}}(\theta)
\ge
W_2^2(p_{\mathrm{tgt}},p_\theta),
\qquad M>0.
\tag{5}
$$

with

$$
W_2^2(p_{\mathrm{tgt}},p_\theta)
=
\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(p_{\mathrm{tgt}},p_\theta)}
\mathbb E_{(x,y)\sim\gamma}\|x-y\|^2.
$$

This is the transport-geometry side of the argument: small CMFM discrepancy controls a Wasserstein gap.

:::caution[Not a new RMFlow theorem]
Equation (5) is imported from prior MeanFlow / flow-map theory. RMFlow's new contribution is the additional likelihood-related control derived below.
:::

---

# 3. Equations (6)–(8): the two-stage noise construction

RMFlow first defines an intermediate noisy target

$$
x_1
=
x_{\mathrm{data}}+\sigma\epsilon_1,
\qquad
\epsilon_1\sim\mathcal N(0,I),
\qquad
\sigma<\sigma_{\min}.
\tag{6}
$$

Then it adds a second Gaussian perturbation

$$
x_{\mathrm{tgt}}
=
x_1+
\sqrt{\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2}\,\epsilon_2,
\qquad
\epsilon_2\sim\mathcal N(0,I).
\tag{7}
$$

Assuming $\epsilon_1\perp\epsilon_2$,

$$
x_{\mathrm{tgt}}
=
x_{\mathrm{data}}
+\sigma\epsilon_1
+\sqrt{\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2}\,\epsilon_2.
$$

Because independent Gaussian covariances add,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}
\left(
\sigma\epsilon_1+
\sqrt{\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2}\,\epsilon_2
\right)
&=
\sigma^2I+(\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2)I\\
&=
\sigma_{\min}^2I.
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence there exists a standard Gaussian $\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$ such that

$$
x_{\mathrm{tgt}}
\overset d=
x_{\mathrm{data}}+\sigma_{\min}\epsilon.
$$

The important identity is therefore

$$
\underbrace{\sigma^2}_{\text{first-stage variance}}
+
\underbrace{(\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2)}_{\text{refinement variance}}
=
\underbrace{\sigma_{\min}^2}_{\text{final variance}}.
$$

## 3.1 Generation equation: Equation (8)

The learned one-step intermediate endpoint is

$$
x_1=x_0+\hat u_{0,1}(x_0;\theta).
$$

Appending the second noise step gives

$$
\hat x_{\mathrm{tgt}}
=
x_0+\hat u_{0,1}(x_0;\theta)
+
\sqrt{\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2}\,\epsilon_2.
\tag{8}
$$

The neural flow is still evaluated only once; the second stage is just Gaussian sampling and addition, so generation remains 1-NFE.

---

# 4. Equation (9): the conditional Gaussian and its log-likelihood

Define

$$
\mu_\theta(x_0)
:=
x_0+\hat u_{0,1}(x_0;\theta),
$$

and

$$
\tau^2
:=
\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2.
$$

Then Equation (8) becomes

$$
\hat x_{\mathrm{tgt}}
=
\mu_\theta(x_0)+\tau\epsilon_2,
\qquad
\epsilon_2\sim\mathcal N(0,I).
$$

Conditioning on $x_0$ makes $\mu_\theta(x_0)$ deterministic, so

$$
\hat x_{\mathrm{tgt}}\mid x_0
\sim
\mathcal N\bigl(
\mu_\theta(x_0),
\tau^2I
\bigr).
$$

Its conditional density is

$$
p_\theta(x_{\mathrm{tgt}}\mid x_0)
=
\frac{1}{(2\pi\tau^2)^{d/2}}
\exp\left[
-\frac{1}{2\tau^2}
\|x_{\mathrm{tgt}}-\mu_\theta(x_0)\|^2
\right].
$$

Taking logs gives

$$
\log p_\theta(x_{\mathrm{tgt}}\mid x_0)
=
-\frac{1}{2(\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2)}
\left\|
 x_{\mathrm{tgt}}-
 \bigl(x_0+\hat u_{0,1}(x_0;\theta)\bigr)
\right\|^2
+C,
\tag{9}
$$

where

$$
C
=-\frac d2
\log\left(2\pi(\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2)\right).
$$

---

# 5. Equation (10): why the NLL becomes an MSE-like objective

Since

$$
x_{\mathrm{tgt}}
\overset d=
x_{\mathrm{data}}+\sigma_{\min}\epsilon,
\qquad
\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I),
$$

and $\tau$ is fixed, the only $\theta$-dependent term in the Gaussian NLL is the squared endpoint error. RMFlow therefore defines

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}
:=
\mathbb E_{x_0,x_{\mathrm{data}},\epsilon}
\left[
\left\|
(x_{\mathrm{data}}+\sigma_{\min}\epsilon)
-
(x_0+\hat u_{0,1}(x_0;\theta))
\right\|^2
\right].
\tag{10}
$$

Strictly, the full Gaussian NLL is

$$
\frac{1}{2\tau^2}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}
+
\frac d2\log(2\pi\tau^2).
$$

Thus Equation (10) is a scaled / shifted surrogate that is optimization-equivalent to Gaussian NLL when the variance is fixed.

---

# 6. Equation (11): linking NLL to marginal likelihood and forward KL

RMFlow's main likelihood relation is

$$
-A\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}+C
\le
\mathbb E_{x_{\mathrm{tgt}}}
[\log p_\theta(x_{\mathrm{tgt}})]
=
-H(p_{\mathrm{tgt}})
-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\mathrm{tgt}}\|p_\theta),
\tag{11}
$$

with

$$
A=
\frac{1}{2(\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2)}.
$$

The remaining equations explain why this bound is valid.

---

# 7. Equation (13): why marginal likelihood is an expectation over $x_0$

For fixed $x_{\mathrm{tgt}}$, $x_0$ is a latent variable:

$$
p_\theta(x_{\mathrm{tgt}})
=
\int q(x_0)
 p_\theta(x_{\mathrm{tgt}}\mid x_0)
\,dx_0
=
\mathbb E_{x_0}
[p_\theta(x_{\mathrm{tgt}}\mid x_0)].
$$

Because $\log$ is concave,

$$
\log\mathbb E[Z]\ge\mathbb E[\log Z].
$$

Setting

$$
Z=p_\theta(x_{\mathrm{tgt}}\mid x_0)
$$

gives

$$
\log p_\theta(x_{\mathrm{tgt}})
\ge
\mathbb E_{x_0}
[\log p_\theta(x_{\mathrm{tgt}}\mid x_0)].
\tag{13}
$$

This is the Jensen lower bound.

---

# 8. Equation (14): why the expectation expands to $(x_0,x_{\mathrm{tgt}})$

Equation (13) holds for every fixed target. Averaging both sides over

$$
x_{\mathrm{tgt}}\sim p_{\mathrm{tgt}}
$$

gives

$$
\mathbb E_{x_{\mathrm{tgt}}}
[\log p_\theta(x_{\mathrm{tgt}})]
\ge
\mathbb E_{x_{\mathrm{tgt}}}
\mathbb E_{x_0}
[\log p_\theta(x_{\mathrm{tgt}}\mid x_0)].
$$

In the unguided product-measure setting,

$$
\mathbb E_{x_{\mathrm{tgt}}}
\mathbb E_{x_0}[\cdot]
=
\mathbb E_{x_0,x_{\mathrm{tgt}}}[\cdot],
$$

so

$$
\mathbb E_{x_{\mathrm{tgt}}}
[\log p_\theta(x_{\mathrm{tgt}})]
\ge
\mathbb E_{x_0,x_{\mathrm{tgt}}}
[\log p_\theta(x_{\mathrm{tgt}}\mid x_0)].
\tag{14}
$$

---

# 9. Equations (15)–(16): substitute the Gaussian likelihood

From Equation (9),

$$
\log p_\theta(x_{\mathrm{tgt}}\mid x_0)
=
-A
\|x_{\mathrm{tgt}}-\mu_\theta(x_0)\|^2
+C.
$$

Therefore

$$
\mathbb E_{x_0,x_{\mathrm{tgt}}}
[\log p_\theta(x_{\mathrm{tgt}}\mid x_0)]
=
-A\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}+C.
\tag{15}
$$

Combining this with Equation (14),

$$
-A\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}+C
\le
\mathbb E_{x_{\mathrm{tgt}}}
[\log p_\theta(x_{\mathrm{tgt}})].
\tag{16}
$$

Hence the precise optimization statement is

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}\downarrow
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{the computable likelihood lower bound increases}.
$$

This does not imply that the true marginal likelihood must increase monotonically after every SGD step.

---

# 10. Equation (17): expected log-likelihood equals $-H-D_{KL}$

Forward KL is

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\mathrm{tgt}}\|p_\theta)
=
\mathbb E_{p_{\mathrm{tgt}}}
\left[
\log\frac{p_{\mathrm{tgt}}(x)}{p_\theta(x)}
\right].
$$

Expanding,

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
=
\mathbb E_{p_{\mathrm{tgt}}}[\log p_{\mathrm{tgt}}]
-
\mathbb E_{p_{\mathrm{tgt}}}[\log p_\theta].
$$

Since

$$
H(p_{\mathrm{tgt}})
=
-\mathbb E_{p_{\mathrm{tgt}}}[\log p_{\mathrm{tgt}}],
$$

we obtain

$$
\mathbb E_{p_{\mathrm{tgt}}}
[\log p_\theta]
=
-H(p_{\mathrm{tgt}})
-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\mathrm{tgt}}\|p_\theta).
\tag{17}
$$

Because $p_{\mathrm{tgt}}$ is fixed, its entropy is constant with respect to $\theta$.

---

# 11. Equation (12): the original RMFlow joint objective

The RMFlow paper optimizes

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{RMFlow}}(\theta,\omega)
=
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CMFM}}
+
\lambda_1\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}
+
\lambda_2
\mathbb E_{(x_{\mathrm{data}},c)}
\|\phi_\omega(c)\|^2.
\tag{12}
$$

| Term | Role in the paper |
|---|---|
| $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CMFM}}$ | MeanFlow path / Wasserstein-style control |
| $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}$ | endpoint likelihood / forward-KL-related control |
| $\|\phi_\omega(c)\|^2$ | guided-prior regularization |

A key factual point is that **the original RMFlow objective uses CMFM, not iMF**. Replacing the first term with iMF is therefore a research extension, not part of the original paper.

# Part II — iMF + RMFlow as a research extension

# 12. Precisely what changes from Original MF to iMF?

Use the iMF interpolation

$$
z_t=(1-t)x+te,
$$

with data sample $x\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}$ and prior noise $e\sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}$.

The sample conditional velocity is

$$
v_c=e-x.
$$

But the state-conditioned marginal velocity is

$$
v(z_t,t)
=
\mathbb E[e-x\mid z_t,t].
\tag{iMF-2}
$$

Therefore the phrase "iMF changes the target to $v_\theta$" is too loose. The precise statement is:

- the **sample supervision remains $e-x$**;
- the **population regression target is the marginal velocity $v(z_t,t)$**;
- iMF changes the **JVP tangent** from sample-specific $e-x$ to state-conditioned $v_\theta(z_t,t)$.

---

# 13. Original MF to the $v$-loss: exact algebraic reformulation

The MeanFlow identity is

$$
 u(z_t,r,t)
=
v(z_t,t)
-(t-r)\frac{d}{dt}u(z_t,r,t).
$$

Equivalently,

$$
v(z_t,t)
=
 u(z_t,r,t)
+(t-r)\frac{d}{dt}u(z_t,r,t).
\tag{iMF-8}
$$

with

$$
\frac{d}{dt}u
=
\operatorname{JVP}(u;v).
$$

Original MF substitutes the sample conditional velocity $e-x$ for the marginal tangent and defines

$$
 u_{\mathrm{tgt}}
=
(e-x)
-(t-r)\operatorname{JVP}(u_\theta;e-x).
\tag{iMF-6}
$$

The original loss is

$$
\mathbb E
\|u_\theta-\operatorname{sg}(u_{\mathrm{tgt}})\|^2.
\tag{iMF-7}
$$

Now define

$$
V_\theta^{\mathrm{MF}}
=
 u_\theta
+(t-r)
\operatorname{JVP}_{\mathrm{sg}}(u_\theta;e-x).
\tag{iMF-9}
$$

Then pointwise

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_\theta^{\mathrm{MF}}-(e-x)
&=
 u_\theta
+(t-r)\operatorname{JVP}_{\mathrm{sg}}(u_\theta;e-x)
-(e-x)\\
&=
 u_\theta-u_{\mathrm{tgt}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore the residuals are identical, and under the same stop-gradient convention the gradients with respect to $\theta$ are identical as well. This is the exact $u$-loss to $v$-loss reformulation used by iMF.

---

# 14. What iMF actually changes: the JVP tangent

Original MF uses

$$
V_\theta^{\mathrm{MF}}(z_t,e-x)
=
 u_\theta(z_t,r,t)
+(t-r)
\operatorname{JVP}_{\mathrm{sg}}(u_\theta;e-x),
$$

which directly depends on the sample-specific quantity $e-x$.

iMF instead defines

$$
V_\theta^{\mathrm{iMF}}(z_t)
=
 u_\theta(z_t,r,t)
+(t-r)
\operatorname{JVP}_{\mathrm{sg}}
\left(u_\theta;v_\theta(z_t,t)\right).
\tag{iMF-12}
$$

The loss is still

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{iMF}}
=
\mathbb E
\|V_\theta^{\mathrm{iMF}}(z_t)-(e-x)\|^2.
$$

The boundary realization used in the paper is

$$
v_\theta(z_t,t)
\equiv
 u_\theta(z_t,t,t),
$$

reflecting the exact boundary relation

$$
v(z_t,t)=u(z_t,t,t).
$$

---

# 15. Conditional-expectation theorem: why labels $e-x$ recover marginal $v$

Let

$$
C=e-x.
$$

For any predictor $F(z_t,t)$ that only depends on state and time, consider

$$
\mathcal R(F)
=
\mathbb E\|F(z_t,t)-C\|^2.
$$

Define

$$
m(z_t,t)
:=
\mathbb E[C\mid z_t,t].
$$

Then the standard orthogonal decomposition gives

$$
\mathbb E\|F-C\|^2
=
\mathbb E\|F-m\|^2
+
\mathbb E\|C-m\|^2.
$$

To see why, write

$$
C=m+\xi,
\qquad
\mathbb E[\xi\mid z_t,t]=0.
$$

Expanding the square yields a cross term

$$
-2\mathbb E[(F-m)^T\xi],
$$

but

$$
\mathbb E[(F-m)^T\xi]
=
\mathbb E\left[
(F-m)^T\mathbb E[\xi\mid z_t,t]
\right]
=0.
$$

Therefore the population MSE minimizer is

$$
F^*(z_t,t)
=
\mathbb E[e-x\mid z_t,t]
=
v(z_t,t).
$$

So the minibatch label may be the noisy conditional velocity $e-x$, while the state-conditioned population target is the marginal velocity.

---

# 16. How far can iMF feasibility be proved rigorously?

Assume exact fields $(u^*,v^*)$ exist, are sufficiently smooth, satisfy the MeanFlow identity

$$
v^*
=
 u^*
+(t-r)\operatorname{JVP}(u^*;v^*),
$$

and are representable by the model class.

If

$$
 u_\theta=u^*,
\qquad
v_\theta=v^*,
$$

then

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_\theta^{\mathrm{iMF}}
&=
 u^*
+(t-r)\operatorname{JVP}(u^*;v^*)\\
&=v^*.
\end{aligned}
$$

Section 15 shows that $v^*$ is the Bayes-optimal state-only MSE predictor. Therefore, under these assumptions, the iMF parameterization **does not exclude the exact MeanFlow solution**.

This is a realizability / population-consistency statement.

:::caution[Not a convergence theorem]
This does not prove that SGD, finite-width networks, finite data, or the stop-gradient training dynamics will converge to $(u^*,v^*)$. Stop-gradient leaves the forward function value unchanged but changes the optimization dynamics.
:::

# 17. The missing layer in the hybrid: RMFlow's first-stage target is $p_\sigma$

This is essential when replacing CMFM with iMF.

RMFlow does not ask the first-stage flow to land directly at

$$
p_{\mathrm{tgt}}
=
p_{\mathrm{data}}*\mathcal N(0,\sigma_{\min}^2I).
$$

Instead, the first stage targets the intermediate distribution

$$
p_\sigma
=
p_{\mathrm{data}}*\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I).
$$

Therefore, if iMF replaces the first stage, its data-side endpoint should be

$$
X_\sigma
=
X_{\mathrm{data}}+\sigma\epsilon_1.
$$

Using the iMF time-direction convention and prior noise $E$,

$$
Z_t
=
(1-t)X_\sigma+tE.
$$

The sample conditional velocity becomes

$$
C_\sigma
=
E-X_\sigma,
$$

and the corresponding marginal velocity is

$$
v_\sigma(z_t,t)
=
\mathbb E[E-X_\sigma\mid Z_t=z_t,t].
$$

Thus a properly matched first-stage iMF objective is

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{iMF}}^{(\sigma)}
=
\mathbb E
\left\|
V_\theta^{\mathrm{iMF}}(Z_t)
-
(E-X_\sigma)
\right\|^2,
$$

with

$$
V_\theta^{\mathrm{iMF}}(Z_t)
=
 u_\theta(Z_t,r,t)
+(t-r)
\operatorname{JVP}_{\mathrm{sg}}
\left(u_\theta;v_\theta(Z_t,t)\right).
$$

This is the correct target correspondence for an iMF first stage inside RMFlow.

---

# 18. Exact distribution-level compatibility

Assume an exact one-step map $G^*$ satisfies

$$
G^*_{\#}q=p_\sigma.
$$

That is, pushing the prior $q$ through $G^*$ exactly produces the intermediate RMFlow distribution.

The second stage adds

$$
\tau\epsilon_2,
\qquad
\tau^2=\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2.
$$

Therefore

$$
p_{\mathrm{final}}
=
p_\sigma*\mathcal N(0,\tau^2I).
$$

Since

$$
p_\sigma
=
p_{\mathrm{data}}*\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I),
$$

we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{final}}
&=
p_{\mathrm{data}}
*\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I)
*\mathcal N(0,\tau^2I)\\
&=
p_{\mathrm{data}}
*\mathcal N(0,(\sigma^2+\tau^2)I)\\
&=
p_{\mathrm{data}}
*\mathcal N(0,\sigma_{\min}^2I)\\
&=
p_{\mathrm{tgt}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, if iMF exactly transports the prior to $p_\sigma$, the RMFlow Gaussian refinement exactly recovers the desired final target distribution.

This statement does not depend on the original CMFM loss; it depends only on the first-stage endpoint distribution being correct.

---

# 19. Approximate learned endpoint: why the RMFlow NLL remains valid

Because iMF and RMFlow use different time / sign conventions, write the learned one-step endpoint generically as

$$
G_\theta(S_0).
$$

In an iMF convention one may write

$$
G_\theta(E)=E-u_\theta(E,0,1),
$$

whereas the RMFlow paper writes

$$
G_\theta(x_0)=x_0+\hat u_{0,1}(x_0;\theta).
$$

The sign convention is irrelevant for the following likelihood argument.

RMFlow defines

$$
Y
=
G_\theta(S_0)+\tau\epsilon_2,
\qquad
\epsilon_2\sim\mathcal N(0,I).
$$

Thus

$$
Y\mid S_0=s
\sim
\mathcal N(G_\theta(s),\tau^2I),
$$

and

$$
\log p_\theta(y\mid s)
=
-\frac{1}{2\tau^2}
\|y-G_\theta(s)\|^2
+C.
$$

This formula does not depend on whether $G_\theta$ was trained by CMFM or iMF.

Define

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{endpoint}}
=
\mathbb E
\left[
\|Y_{\mathrm{tgt}}-G_\theta(S_0)\|^2
\right],
$$

with

$$
Y_{\mathrm{tgt}}
\overset d=
X_{\mathrm{data}}+\sigma_{\min}\epsilon.
$$

The same Jensen argument gives

$$
-\frac{1}{2\tau^2}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{endpoint}}
+C
\le
\mathbb E_{Y\sim p_{\mathrm{tgt}}}
[\log p_\theta(Y)].
$$

Therefore the RMFlow likelihood lower-bound mechanism survives the replacement of the first-stage training objective by iMF.

# 20. Proposed iMF + RMFlow objective

A natural research objective is

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{iMF+RM}}
=
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{iMF}}^{(\sigma)}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{NLL}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{endpoint}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{guide}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{guide}}.
$$

This differs from RMFlow Equation (12):

$$
\underbrace{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CMFM}}}_{\text{original RMFlow}}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\underbrace{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{iMF}}^{(\sigma)}}_{\text{proposed replacement}}.
$$

So this must be presented as a proposal / research extension, not as an equation already established by the RMFlow paper.

# 21. What is proved, what requires assumptions, and what remains open?

## 21.1 Established by the original RMFlow derivation

- the noise construction in Equations (6)–(8);
- the conditional Gaussian likelihood in Equation (9);
- the NLL surrogate in Equation (10);
- the Jensen / marginal-likelihood / KL chain in Equations (11), (13)–(17);
- the original joint objective in Equation (12), which uses CMFM + NLL + guidance regularization.

## 21.2 Derivable here under explicit assumptions

- Original MF's $u$-loss and the iMF paper's $v$-loss reformulation are algebraically equivalent under the stated stop-gradient convention;
- state-only MSE regression on sample label $e-x$ has marginal velocity $v(z_t,t)$ as its population target;
- if exact $u^*,v^*$ are representable, the iMF compound parameterization can represent the Bayes-optimal marginal velocity;
- if the iMF first stage exactly transports the prior to $p_\sigma$, RMFlow's second Gaussian stage exactly yields $p_{\mathrm{tgt}}$;
- for an approximate learned endpoint map, the RMFlow Gaussian NLL / Jensen lower bound remains valid.

## 21.3 Still not proved

The following statements should not be promoted to theorems:

1. SGD training of iMF necessarily converges to exact $u^*,v^*$;
2. finite networks / finite data necessarily produce the correct intermediate distribution $p_\sigma$;
3. the original RMFlow Wasserstein result
   $$
   M\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CMFM}}\ge W_2^2
   $$
   remains valid after simply replacing $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CMFM}}$ with $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{iMF}}$;
4. the hybrid objective necessarily improves FID, KL, or perceptual quality.

The main theoretical gap is therefore a stability theorem of the form

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{iMF}}^{(\sigma)}
\Longrightarrow
\text{endpoint-map error}
\Longrightarrow
W_2\text{ control}.
$$

# 22. Final target map: keep the three levels separate

| Level | Quantity | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| sample supervision | $e-x$; in the hybrid, $e-X_\sigma$ | Monte Carlo conditional-velocity label |
| population velocity target | $v(z_t,t)=\mathbb E[e-x\mid z_t,t]$ | state-conditioned marginal velocity |
| iMF JVP tangent | $v_\theta(z_t,t)$ | network estimate of the marginal velocity |
| RMFlow first-stage endpoint | $G_\theta(S_0)$ | one-step deterministic transport result |
| RMFlow likelihood target | $X_{\mathrm{data}}+\sigma_{\min}\epsilon$ | final noisy target distribution |

The precise statement is therefore:

> **iMF keeps the sample supervision but replaces the sample-specific JVP tangent by a state-conditioned estimate $v_\theta$. If iMF is used as RMFlow's first stage, the sample supervision should be matched to the intermediate target $X_\sigma$, i.e. $e-X_\sigma$, because the first stage is supposed to transport into $p_\sigma$, not directly into the final $p_{\mathrm{tgt}}$.**
