# 0. Begin with the object RMFlow creates

For one prior sample $x_0$, iMF / MeanFlow produces a deterministic one-step endpoint

$$
\mu_\theta(x_0)=x_0+u_\theta(x_0,0,1).
$$

RMFlow turns that point into a conditional distribution by adding Gaussian refinement noise:

$$
Y=\mu_\theta(x_0)+\tau\varepsilon,
\qquad \varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I).
$$

Thus

$$
Y\mid x_0\sim\mathcal N(\mu_\theta(x_0),\tau^2I).
$$

# 1. Variance composition

With

$$
X_1=X_{\mathrm{data}}+\sigma\varepsilon_1
$$

and

$$
X_{\mathrm{tgt}}=X_1+\sqrt{\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2}\,\varepsilon_2,
$$

independence gives

$$
\operatorname{Cov}(X_{\mathrm{tgt}}\mid X_{\mathrm{data}})
=\sigma^2I+(\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2)I
=\sigma_{\min}^2I.
$$

Therefore

$$
X_{\mathrm{tgt}}\overset d=
X_{\mathrm{data}}+\sigma_{\min}\varepsilon.
$$

# 2. Gaussian NLL

Let

$$
\tau^2=\sigma_{\min}^2-\sigma^2.
$$

Then

$$
-\log p_\theta(y\mid x_0)
=
\frac{\|y-\mu_\theta(x_0)\|^2}{2\tau^2}
+\frac d2\log(2\pi\tau^2).
$$

For fixed $\tau$, minimizing NLL is equivalent to minimizing the squared endpoint error.

# 3. Jensen lower bound

The marginal density is

$$
p_\theta(y)=\int q(x_0)p_\theta(y\mid x_0)dx_0.
$$

Since $\log$ is concave,

$$
\log p_\theta(y)
\ge
\mathbb E_{X_0}[\log p_\theta(y\mid X_0)].
$$

Averaging over $Y\sim p_{\mathrm{tgt}}$ yields

$$
-A\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}+C
\le
\mathbb E_Y[\log p_\theta(Y)],
\qquad
A=\frac{1}{2\tau^2}>0.
$$

Finally,

$$
\mathbb E_{p_{\mathrm{tgt}}}[\log p_\theta(Y)]
=-H(p_{\mathrm{tgt}})
-D_{KL}(p_{\mathrm{tgt}}\|p_\theta).
$$

# 4. Rigorous iMF compatibility proposition

Assume a measurable deterministic one-step map $g_\theta$, fixed $\tau>0$, independent $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$, and

$$
Y=g_\theta(X_0)+\tau\varepsilon.
$$

Then

$$
Y\mid X_0=x_0
\sim\mathcal N(g_\theta(x_0),\tau^2I),
$$

and consequently

$$
-\frac1{2\tau^2}
\mathbb E\|Y-g_\theta(X_0)\|^2+C
\le
\mathbb E_Y[\log p_\theta(Y)].
$$

The proof only uses the final map and Gaussian refinement; it does not use the training-time JVP tangent.

For Improved MeanFlow, set

$$
g_\theta(x_0)=x_0+u_\theta(x_0,0,1).
$$

Therefore replacing the original sample-specific JVP tangent with $v_\theta$ does not invalidate the RMFlow likelihood proof.

If $g_\theta$ is differentiable in $\theta$ and differentiation can be exchanged with expectation,

$$
\nabla_\theta
\mathbb E\|Y-g_\theta(X_0)\|^2
=
2\mathbb E\left[
J_{g_\theta}(X_0)^T(g_\theta(X_0)-Y)
\right],
$$

so the endpoint term directly updates the one-step inference map.

# 5. What this does not prove

This compatibility result does **not** imply

$$
M\mathcal L_{\mathrm{iMF}}\ge W_2^2
$$

because the original Wasserstein-style theorem is tied to a specific CMFM discrepancy. A separate comparison or stability theorem is required.

It also does not prove that lowering endpoint NLL must lower FID, nor that the true marginal likelihood improves monotonically at every optimizer step. Those are empirical / theoretical questions beyond the compatibility proposition.

For the full English derivation of every expectation and KL step, see [RMFlow paper notes](/research-handbook/en/papers/rmflow/).
