# Flow Matching → MeanFlow → Improved MeanFlow

This narrative centers on one question: **how can a generative ODE move from repeated local velocity queries toward effective one-step generation?**

---

## 1. Why does Flow Matching usually need multiple NFEs?

Flow Matching learns an instantaneous velocity field:

$$
\frac{dz_t}{dt}=v_\theta(z_t,t).
$$

During inference, the state moves from noise at $t=1$ toward data at $t=0$. As the state changes, the local direction may change, so the model is usually queried again.

This multi-NFE behavior follows directly from the semantics of the predicted quantity: it describes **local / instantaneous** dynamics.

For the linear path

$$
z_t=(1-t)x+te
$$

a sampled pair provides

$$
e-x.
$$

This is a **conditional velocity**. Under MSE regression, the unique marginal field is

$$
v(z_t,t)=\mathbb E[e-x\mid z_t,t].
$$

See the [Flow Matching reference](/research-handbook/en/foundations/flow-matching/) for the underlying definitions.

---

## 2. MeanFlow: from instantaneous velocity to average velocity

For direct transport from $z_1$ to $z_0$, one can instead model the average velocity over the whole interval.

MeanFlow defines

$$
u(z_t,r,t)=\frac{1}{t-r}\int_r^t v(z_\tau,\tau)\,d\tau.
$$

Then

$$
z_r=z_t-(t-r)u(z_t,r,t).
$$

For one-step generation:

$$
(r,t)=(0,1),\qquad z_0=z_1-u_\theta(z_1,0,1).
$$

This is the central representation shift:

**instantaneous velocity $v$ → interval-average velocity $u$.**

---

## 3. Where does the training target for average velocity come from?

The sample-level quantity $e-x$ is easy to obtain, but there is no equally direct per-sample ground-truth label for $u(z_t,r,t)$.

MeanFlow therefore requires an identity connecting the **average quantity $u$** to the **local quantity $v$**.

<DerivationStepper lang="en" />

The derivation gives

$$
v=u+(t-r)\frac{du}{dt},
$$

with

$$
\frac{du}{dt}=\partial_z u\,v+\partial_tu,
$$

which can be computed with a JVP.

The JVP represents the total derivative of the average velocity $u$ along state and time; it is not a separate velocity field.

---

## 4. The Original MeanFlow training objective

The theoretical identity requires the marginal velocity $v(z_t,t)$. Original MeanFlow uses the sampled conditional velocity $e-x$ as the JVP tangent:

$$
u_{tgt}=(e-x)-(t-r)\operatorname{JVP}(u_\theta;e-x).
$$

and trains

$$
\mathcal L=\mathbb E\|u_\theta-\operatorname{sg}(u_{tgt})\|^2.
$$

Two points are distinct:

1. The semantics of $u_\theta$ remain average velocity.
2. $e-x$ is used not only as supervision but also inside the JVP tangent, so it enters the target-construction / predictor pathway.

---

## 5. Improved MeanFlow objective reformulation

<MFvsIMFBridge lang="en" />

Original MF can be rewritten as an equivalent v-loss:

$$
V_\theta=u_\theta+(t-r)\operatorname{JVP}_{sg}(u_\theta;e-x),
$$

$$
\mathcal L=\mathbb E\|V_\theta-(e-x)\|^2.
$$

The compound predictor can then be expressed as

$$
V_\theta=V_\theta(z_t,e-x).
$$

Improved MeanFlow replaces the JVP tangent with a marginal-like prediction:

$$
V_\theta(z_t)=u_\theta(z_t)+(t-r)\operatorname{JVP}_{sg}(u_\theta;v_\theta).
$$

while the supervision remains

$$
\mathcal L=\mathbb E\|V_\theta(z_t)-(e-x)\|^2.
$$

The objective-level distinction is therefore:

**Original MF: the JVP tangent uses $e-x$.**  
**Improved MF: the JVP tangent uses $v_\theta(z_t)$, while $e-x$ remains the supervision signal.**

---

## 6. Objective change vs system-level result

The objective reformulation has relatively controlled experimental evidence:

| Setting | 1-NFE FID ↓ |
|---|---:|
| Original MF-B/2 | 6.17 |
| iMF boundary $v_\theta=u_\theta(z_t,t,t)$ | 5.97 |
| iMF auxiliary $v$-head | 5.68 |
| Original MF-XL/2 | 3.43 |
| MF-XL/2 + iMF boundary objective | 2.99 |

Improved MeanFlow also adds:

- flexible $\omega$ conditioning
- $\Omega=\{\omega,t_{min},t_{max}\}$ conditioning
- in-context conditioning
- SwiGLU / RMSNorm / RoPE
- longer training

The final system-level iMF-XL/2 result reaches **FID 1.72**.

Accordingly, the evidence for the objective reformulation should be distinguished from the final result obtained after multiple system-level changes are combined.

---

## 7. Original MeanFlow vs Improved MeanFlow

| Question | Original MeanFlow | Improved MeanFlow |
|---|---|---|
| Core goal | One-step generation with average velocity | Improve the MeanFlow objective and strengthen guidance / conditioning |
| Inference object | $u_\theta$ | same $u_\theta$ |
| One-step inference | $z_0=z_1-u_\theta(z_1,0,1)$ | **unchanged** |
| Sample supervision | $e-x$ | **unchanged** |
| JVP tangent | $e-x$ | $v_\theta(z_t)$ |
| Objective concern | network-dependent target / sample-specific tangent | state-conditioned compound predictor |
| Additional system changes | fixed CFG | flexible CFG + conditioning / architecture changes |

## 8. Open questions

Several questions remain open after MeanFlow and iMF:

- Does learning difficulty change systematically with interval length?
- What dominates one-step endpoint error: representation, optimization, or local derivative estimation?
- Does JVP / gradient geometry amplify errors in particular time regions?
- How should multi-step variants trade accuracy against compute?
- How can average-flow or flow-map representations extend to robotics and action generation?
