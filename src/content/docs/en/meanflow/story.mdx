# The whole story first

This path asks one question: **how do we move from many local velocity queries in a generative ODE to effective one-step generation?**

---

## 1. Why does Flow Matching usually need multiple NFEs?

Flow Matching learns an instantaneous velocity field:

$$
\frac{dz_t}{dt}=v_\theta(z_t,t).
$$

During inference, we move from noise at $t=1$ toward data at $t=0$. Once the state changes, the local direction can change as well, so the model is queried again.

The multi-NFE requirement is therefore tied to the semantics of the predicted quantity: it is **local / instantaneous**.

For the linear path

$$z_t=(1-t)x+te,$$

a sampled pair provides

$$e-x,$$

but this is a **conditional velocity**. The unique marginal field is

$$
v(z_t,t)=\mathbb E[e-x\mid z_t,t].
$$

---

## 2. MeanFlow's first idea: stop predicting only local velocity

If we want to move from $z_1$ to $z_0$ in one step, the natural question is:

> Can we directly learn the average velocity over the whole interval?

MeanFlow defines

$$
u(z_t,r,t)=\frac{1}{t-r}\int_r^t v(z_\tau,\tau)\,d\tau.
$$

Then

$$
z_r=z_t-(t-r)u(z_t,r,t).
$$

For one-step generation:

$$
(r,t)=(0,1),\qquad z_0=z_1-u_\theta(z_1,0,1).
$$

This is the core representation shift:

**instantaneous velocity $v$ → interval-average velocity $u$.**

---

## 3. But where does the ground-truth average velocity come from?

We can cheaply sample $e-x$, but we do not have an equally convenient per-sample regression target for $u(z_t,r,t)$.

MeanFlow therefore needs an identity that connects the **average quantity $u$** to the **local quantity $v$**.

<DerivationStepper lang="en" />

The result is

$$
v=u+(t-r)\frac{du}{dt},
$$

with

$$
\frac{du}{dt}=\partial_z u\,v+\partial_tu,
$$

which can be computed as a JVP.

> The JVP is not an arbitrary implementation trick. It appears because MeanFlow learns an average quantity that does not come with a simple sampled ground-truth label.

---

## 4. How does Original MeanFlow turn the identity into a training objective?

The theoretical identity requires the marginal velocity $v(z_t,t)$. Original MeanFlow uses the sampled conditional velocity $e-x$ as the JVP tangent:

$$
u_{tgt}=(e-x)-(t-r)\operatorname{JVP}(u_\theta;e-x),$$

then trains

$$
\mathcal L=\mathbb E\|u_\theta-\operatorname{sg}(u_{tgt})\|^2.
$$

Two distinctions matter:

1. **$u_\theta$ is not predicting $e-x$.** Its semantics remain average velocity.
2. **$e-x$ is not only supervision.** It also enters the JVP tangent and therefore the prediction/target-construction path.

---

## 5. What exactly does Improved MeanFlow change?

Ignore CFG, architecture, and the final 1.72 FID for a moment. First isolate the objective-level change.

<MFvsIMFBridge lang="en" />

Rewrite Original MF as an equivalent v-loss:

$$
V_\theta=u_\theta+(t-r)\operatorname{JVP}_{sg}(u_\theta;e-x),
$$

$$
\mathcal L=\mathbb E\|V_\theta-(e-x)\|^2.
$$

Now the issue is visible:

$$V_\theta=V_\theta(z_t,e-x).$$

Improved MeanFlow changes the JVP tangent to a marginal-like prediction:

$$
V_\theta(z_t)=u_\theta(z_t)+(t-r)\operatorname{JVP}_{sg}(u_\theta;v_\theta).
$$

The supervision is still

$$
\mathcal L=\mathbb E\|V_\theta(z_t)-(e-x)\|^2.
$$

### One-line memory aid

> **Original MF: $e-x$ enters both supervision and the JVP.**  
> **iMF: $e-x$ stays only in supervision; the JVP uses $v_\theta(z_t)$.**

---

## 6. Why does the final iMF system reach FID 1.72?

Do not attribute the entire gain to the objective change.

Controlled objective evidence includes:

| Setting | 1-NFE FID ↓ |
|---|---:|
| Original MF-B/2 | 6.17 |
| iMF boundary $v_\theta=u_\theta(z_t,t,t)$ | 5.97 |
| iMF auxiliary $v$-head | 5.68 |
| Original MF-XL/2 | 3.43 |
| MF-XL/2 + iMF boundary objective | 2.99 |

The iMF system also adds flexible guidance conditioning, $\Omega$-conditioning, in-context conditioning, Transformer block changes, and longer training. The final iMF-XL/2 system reaches **FID 1.72**.

The correct interpretation is:

> **The objective reformulation has independent supporting evidence, but the final 1.72 result is a system-level outcome.**

---

## 7. The relationship between the two papers in one table

| Question | Original MeanFlow | Improved MeanFlow |
|---|---|---|
| Main goal | One-step generation with average velocity | Stabilize/clean up MeanFlow training and improve guidance/conditioning |
| Inference object | $u_\theta$ | same $u_\theta$ |
| One-step inference | $z_0=z_1-u_\theta(z_1,0,1)$ | **unchanged** |
| Sample supervision | $e-x$ | **unchanged** |
| JVP tangent | $e-x$ | $v_\theta(z_t)$ |
| Training concern | network-dependent target / sample-specific tangent | state-only compound predictor $V_\theta(z_t)$ |
| Additional system changes | fixed CFG | flexible CFG + conditioning/architecture changes |

---

## 8. What research questions remain?

After iMF, simply re-identifying the original network-dependent target is not enough novelty. More promising directions include temporal difficulty, endpoint error, JVP/gradient geometry, two-step intermediate constraints, and transferring average-flow or flow-map representations to robotics/action generation.
