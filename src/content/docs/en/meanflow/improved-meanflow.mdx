## 0. The core issue is not inventing another velocity

Original MeanFlow already supports one-step inference. iMF primarily addresses the training objective and guidance flexibility. The key objective observation is that original MF uses the sample-specific $e-x$ as the JVP tangent, so the recast compound predictor depends on a conditional training-sample quantity.

## 1. Separate the symbols first

<IMFNotationExplorer lang="en" />

In particular,

$$
\operatorname{JVP}(u_\theta;v_\theta)
$$

does not mean taking a JVP of $v_\theta$. The differentiated function is $u_\theta$; lowercase $v_\theta$ supplies the tangent direction. The JVP output is then combined with $u_\theta$ to form capital $V_\theta$.

## 2. Original MF as a v-loss

The MeanFlow Identity is

$$
v=u+(t-r)\frac{du}{dt}.
$$

Original MF can therefore be recast with the compound predictor

$$
V_\theta(z_t,e-x)
=u_\theta(z_t,r,t)
+(t-r)\operatorname{JVP}_{sg}(u_\theta;e-x),
$$

and the regression loss

$$
\mathcal L=\mathbb E\|V_\theta(z_t,e-x)-(e-x)\|^2.
$$

The prediction pathway itself now visibly depends on $e-x$.

## 3. The iMF replacement

iMF parameterizes a marginal-like velocity prediction $v_\theta(z_t,t)$ and uses it as the JVP tangent:

$$
V_\theta(z_t)
=u_\theta(z_t,r,t)
+(t-r)\operatorname{JVP}_{sg}(u_\theta;v_\theta).
$$

Supervision remains

$$
\mathcal L_{iMF}=\mathbb E\|V_\theta(z_t)-(e-x)\|^2.
$$

:::caution[What does not change]
iMF does **not** remove $e-x$ from the loss. It removes $e-x$ from the JVP tangent / predictor pathway.
:::

## 4. What the JVP contains

For input $(z,r,t)$, the iMF tangent is

$$
[v_\theta,0,1],
$$

so

$$
\operatorname{JVP}(u_\theta;v_\theta)
=J_{u_\theta}[v_\theta,0,1]
=\partial_z u_\theta\,v_\theta+\partial_tu_\theta.
$$

The role of $v_\theta$ is therefore precise: it selects the state-space direction along which the Jacobian is projected.

## 5. Where lowercase $v_\theta$ comes from

The boundary variant uses

$$
v_\theta(z_t,t)\equiv u_\theta(z_t,t,t),
$$

which adds no parameters. The auxiliary-head variant predicts $v_\theta$ with a training-only head and an additional Flow Matching loss $\|v_\theta-(e-x)\|^2$; inference still uses only $u_\theta$.

## 6. Why this can reduce variance

For a fixed $z_t$, different sampled pairs may yield different conditional velocities $e-x$. A Jacobian-vector product can transmit and magnify this tangent variance into the correction term. iMF instead uses the state-conditioned $v_\theta(z_t)$, yielding a legitimate state-conditioned compound regression function $V_\theta(z_t)$.

## 7. Keep objective and system changes separate

Beyond the objective reformulation, iMF adds flexible guidance conditioning, $\Omega$-conditioning, in-context conditioning, Transformer block changes, and longer training. The final FID 1.72 is therefore a system-level result.
