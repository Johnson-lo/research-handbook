## Core idea

iMF keeps MeanFlow's **average-velocity representation** and one-step inference. It changes how that representation is trained.

Rearrange the MeanFlow identity:

$$
v=u+(t-r)\frac{du}{dt}.
$$

### Original MF as an equivalent v-loss

Define

$$
V_\theta=u_\theta+(t-r)\operatorname{JVP}_{sg}(u_\theta;e-x).
$$

Then the original objective can be viewed equivalently as

$$
\mathcal L=\mathbb E\|V_\theta-(e-x)\|^2.
$$

This exposes a new problem: the predictor is effectively $V_\theta(z_t,e-x)$ because the sample-specific conditional velocity also enters the JVP tangent.

## iMF modification

iMF replaces that tangent with a model estimate of the marginal velocity:

$$
V_\theta(z_t)=u_\theta(z_t)+(t-r)\operatorname{JVP}_{sg}(u_\theta;v_\theta).
$$

The supervision remains

$$
\mathcal L_{iMF}=\mathbb E\|V_\theta(z_t)-(e-x)\|^2.
$$

:::caution[What did not disappear]
iMF does **not** remove $e-x$ from supervision. It removes $e-x$ from the JVP / prediction pathway.
:::

## Where does $v_\theta$ come from?

Boundary condition:

$$
v(z_t,t)=u(z_t,t,t),
$$

so the same network can define

$$
v_\theta(z_t,t)\equiv u_\theta(z_t,t,t).
$$

The paper also studies an auxiliary $v$-head used during training.

## Training vs inference

**Training:** sample $x,e,t,r$, construct $z_t$, compute $u_\theta$, obtain $v_\theta$, evaluate JVP, form $V_\theta$, compare against $e-x$.

**Inference:** no real $x$. Sample $z_1\sim p_{prior}$ and perform

$$z_0=z_1-u_\theta(z_1,0,1).$$

## Beyond the objective

iMF also introduces flexible CFG conditioning and in-context conditioning so guidance settings can be changed at inference instead of being fixed during training.
