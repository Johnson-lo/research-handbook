# From zero to the calculus used by MeanFlow

This page covers only the multivariable-calculus ideas actually needed for MeanFlow.

<CalculusJVPPrimer lang="en" />

## 1. Why partial derivatives appear

The MeanFlow average velocity depends on several inputs:

$$
u=u(z_t,r,t).
$$

When asking how $u$ changes with $t$, two things happen: $t$ changes directly, and the state $z_t$ also moves. Therefore the relevant quantity is the total derivative

$$
\frac{du}{dt}=\partial_z u\,\frac{dz_t}{dt}+\partial_tu.
$$

Using the flow ODE $dz_t/dt=v(z_t,t)$ gives

$$
\frac{du}{dt}=\partial_z u\,v+\partial_tu.
$$

## 2. What is the Jacobian?

When both $z$ and $u$ are high-dimensional vectors, $\partial_z u$ is a Jacobian matrix. MeanFlow does not need to materialize the entire matrix. It only needs the change in $u$ along the flow direction $v$, namely $J_u v$.

That quantity is the Jacobian-vector product (JVP).

## 3. Why is the tangent $[v,0,1]$?

For the full input $(z,r,t)$, the state moves with tangent $v$, the left endpoint $r$ is held fixed, and time moves with tangent $1$. Therefore

$$
\operatorname{JVP}(u;v)=J_{u(z,r,t)}[v,0,1]=\partial_z u\,v+\partial_tu.
$$

This is the mathematical meaning of code such as `jvp(fn, (z,r,t), (v,0,1))`.

## 4. A notation warning for iMF

In

$$
\operatorname{JVP}(u_\theta;v_\theta),
$$

the differentiated function is $u_\theta$. The quantity $v_\theta$ is the state-space tangent direction; it is not the function being differentiated.

The JVP output is then combined with $u_\theta$ to form capital $V_\theta$:

$$
V_\theta=u_\theta+(t-r)\operatorname{JVP}_{sg}(u_\theta;v_\theta).
$$
