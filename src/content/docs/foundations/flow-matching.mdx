From

$$z_t=(1-t)x+te$$

we obtain

$$\frac{dz_t}{dt}=e-x.$$

For a particular sampled pair $(x,e)$, $e-x$ is the **conditional instantaneous velocity**.

However, the same $(z_t,t)$ can be compatible with many different pairs $(x,e)$. Therefore the unique MSE regression optimum is

$$
v(z_t,t)=\mathbb E[e-x\mid z_t,t].
$$

## Three “GT” levels that should not be conflated

| Level | Quantity | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Sample supervision | $e-x$ | Conditional velocity for one sampled pair |
| Unique instantaneous field | $v(z,t)$ | Marginal velocity, the MSE regression optimum |
| MeanFlow theoretical field | $u(z,r,t)$ | Average velocity across an interval |

Flow Matching trains

$$
\mathcal L_{FM}=\mathbb E\|v_\theta(z_t,t)-(e-x)\|^2.
$$

At inference time, it numerically solves $dz_t/dt=v_\theta(z_t,t)$ from $t=1$ to $t=0$, typically requiring multiple NFEs.
